#1
Food
NUTRITION & PRECISION FUELING

Food is the most direct metabolic signal you send your body dozens of times per day. Every meal either worsens or improves insulin sensitivity, mitochondrial efficiency, and systemic inflammation. The metabolic crisis isn’t caused by eating too much — it’s caused by eating the wrong foods in the wrong amounts at the wrong times, producing hormonal signals that lock your body into fat storage mode and out of fat-burning mode. Precision nutrition restores the conversation between your cells and your insulin.

◆ PROTOCOL-SPECIFIC ACTION
Begin with a 2-week elimination of all industrial seed oils (canola, soybean, corn, cottonseed), refined sugars, and ultra-processed foods. This alone reduces the primary inflammatory drivers within days — before any other intervention begins.

CORE DIETARY PRINCIPLES

  • Protein First: 1.6–2.2g/kg body weight daily. Protein is the most insulin-stable macronutrient and the strongest signal for lean mass preservation and satiety. High-quality sources: pasture-raised eggs, wild salmon, grass-fed beef, sardines, organ meats, Greek yogurt, legumes for those who tolerate them.
  • Carbohydrate Quality over Quantity: Remove all refined carbohydrates and fructose (the only carbohydrate metabolized exclusively by the liver, driving hepatic fat and VLDL). Keep carbohydrates from whole-food sources: berries, leafy greens, cruciferous vegetables, sweet potato, resistant starch.
  • Fat Quality is Paramount: Eliminate seed oils (PUFAs oxidize under heat creating lipid peroxides). Prioritize: extra-virgin olive oil (oleocanthal as anti-inflammatory), avocado, coconut oil/MCT (direct mitochondrial fuel via ketones), grass-fed butter/ghee, fatty fish (EPA/DHA).
  • Time-Restricted Eating (TRE): Compress eating window to 8–10 hours daily (e.g., 10am–6pm). This alone improves insulin sensitivity, AMPK activation, and autophagy independent of caloric restriction. Start with 12:12 if 16:8 feels too aggressive. Studies show TRE lowers fasting insulin, HbA1c, and blood pressure within 5 weeks.
  • Metabolic Flexibility Training — Ketogenic Phases: 4–8 week therapeutic ketogenic phases (<30g net carbs/day) are highly effective for rapid insulin reset, visceral fat mobilization, and mitochondrial biogenesis. Not permanent — cyclical keto then carb cycling maintains flexibility without hormonal suppression.
  • Resistant Starch: Cooked-and-cooled potatoes, rice, green bananas, plantains. Resistant starch feeds butyrate-producing bacteria, improves insulin sensitivity, and lowers postprandial glucose. One of the most underutilized metabolic tools.
  • Meal Sequence Matters: Eat fiber and protein before carbohydrates in a meal. Studies show this sequence reduces postprandial glucose by up to 73% (Shukla et al., DMCARE 2015).
  • Food Timing & Circadian Alignment: Front-load calories earlier in the day. Late-night eating (after 8pm) drives insulin resistance independent of what is eaten. The circadian liver and pancreas are far less insulin-sensitive at night.
  • Anti-inflammatory Foods to Prioritize Daily: Fatty fish (EPA/DHA), extra-virgin olive oil, turmeric + black pepper, ginger, cruciferous vegetables (sulforaphane activates Nrf2), berries (polyphenols activate AMPK), green tea (EGCG).
  • Foods to Eliminate: Industrial seed oils (canola, soybean, corn, safflower, sunflower, cottonseed), high-fructose corn syrup, refined grains, artificial sweeteners (disrupt glucose sensing), processed deli meats (nitrates + advanced glycation end products).
  • Fasting Mimicking Diet (FMD — Valter Longo, Ph.D.): A 5-day per month protocol (typically 800–1100 kcal/day from plant-based, low-protein, low-glycemic foods) that mimics the physiological effects of prolonged fasting while allowing food intake. Developed by Dr. Valter Longo at USC, FMD activates autophagy, reduces IGF-1, lowers fasting glucose and insulin, reduces visceral fat, and has demonstrated regenerative effects on immune and stem cell populations. Clinical trials (ProLon protocol) show significant reductions in metabolic risk factors after 3 monthly cycles. Particularly powerful for the Type A and Type E metabolic phenotypes. Can be done with the commercial ProLon kit or replicated with careful food selection.

FEMALE NOTE

Women cycling through perimenopause do better with carb cycling (5 low-carb days, 2 moderate-carb days) than strict ketosis. The luteal phase benefits from slightly higher complex carbohydrates to support serotonin and progesterone. Rigid restriction in women can suppress LH, FSH, and thyroid — metabolic rigidity is not the goal.

#2
Movement
EXERCISE & METABOLIC ADAPTATION

Skeletal muscle is the largest insulin-sensitive tissue in the human body — responsible for up to 70–80% of glucose disposal after meals. Every pound of muscle is a metabolic sink. Movement adds nearly 7 years to life expectancy and improves virtually every metabolic biomarker independently of diet. Zone 2 cardio and resistance training together are more powerful than any pharmaceutical for metabolic reset when done consistently.

◆ PROTOCOL-SPECIFIC ACTION
Begin with 10-minute post-meal walks within 30 minutes of eating. A 10-minute walk after meals reduces postprandial glucose by up to 22% — this is the lowest-barrier, highest-yield metabolic intervention available to anyone, anywhere, for free.

THE MOVEMENT STACK FOR METABOLIC RESET

  • Zone 2 Cardio (Foundation): 3–4 sessions/week, 45–60 min each. Zone 2 = 60–70% max heart rate, conversational pace (e.g., brisk walk where you can hold a full conversation, easy cycling, light swimming, elliptical, conversational-pace jog). Zone 2 preferentially burns fat, builds mitochondrial density, improves fat oxidation capacity, and raises lactate threshold. This is the metabolic base everything else builds on.
  • Resistance Training (Non-Negotiable): 2–4 sessions/week. Muscle is the #1 metabolic organ. Every 10% increase in muscle mass produces a measurable improvement in insulin sensitivity. Compound movements (deadlift, squat, row, press) recruit the largest glucose-disposal muscle groups. Prioritize heavy loads over high reps for myofibrillar hypertrophy. Muscle loss with age (sarcopenia) is the most underdiagnosed driver of late-life metabolic disease.
  • Post-Meal Walks: 10–15 min walks within 30 min of eating. Non-negotiable daily practice. Activates GLUT4 transporters in muscle via insulin-independent mechanism — glucose enters muscle without requiring insulin. This is especially powerful for people with significant insulin resistance.
  • HIIT (Strategic Use): 1–2 sessions/week max. High-intensity intervals (20 sec on, 40 sec recovery ×8–10) are time-efficient and potent AMPK activators. However, HIIT without a Zone 2 base drives cortisol elevation and can worsen metabolic function in already-stressed individuals. Build Zone 2 first for 4–6 weeks before adding HIIT.
  • NEAT (Non-Exercise Activity Thermogenesis): Often exceeds structured exercise in total daily caloric expenditure. Standing desk, walking during calls, taking stairs, parking farther. Target: 7,000–10,000 steps/day total. Sedentary time >6 hrs/day independently drives insulin resistance even in people who exercise regularly.
  • Cold Exposure Post-Workout: Cold showers (2–3 min cold finish) activate brown adipose tissue (BAT) thermogenesis, increase PGC-1α (mitochondrial biogenesis), and improve glucose disposal. Avoid cold immediately after resistance training — wait 4+ hrs to allow anabolic signaling to complete.
  • Grip Strength as Metabolic Proxy: Track grip strength as a metabolic health metric — it correlates directly with insulin sensitivity, lean mass, and all-cause mortality. Aim for improvement over time as a tangible biomarker of metabolic gains.

The ideal week: Zone 2 (Mon/Wed/Fri), Resistance (Tue/Thu), Post-meal walks (daily), 1 HIIT session once base is established.

#3
Sleep & Light
CIRCADIAN METABOLIC MASTER

A single night of 4–5 hours of sleep produces insulin resistance equivalent to early type 2 diabetes — in healthy people. Chronic sleep deprivation is one of the most powerful metabolic disruptors known. Growth hormone (secreted in deep sleep stages) is the primary fat-mobilizing hormone. Without adequate deep sleep, no protocol — dietary or pharmaceutical — works at full capacity. Light is the master synchronizer of your metabolic clock.

◆ PROTOCOL-SPECIFIC ACTION
Get 10–20 minutes of outdoor light exposure within 60 minutes of waking. No sunglasses. This single action anchors your circadian clock, synchronizes cortisol peak with morning (where it belongs), improves nighttime melatonin, and improves insulin sensitivity the same day — no supplementation required.
  • Target 7.5–9 hours: Most metabolic repair, glymphatic clearance, and GH secretion happens in hours 7–9 of sleep. Consistently under 7 hours = measurably elevated fasting glucose the next morning.
  • Morning Sunlight Protocol: 10–20 min outdoor light within 1 hr of waking (cloudy = still works, 10,000–50,000 lux outdoors vs. 500 lux indoors). Sets the cortisol awakening response, synchronizes all peripheral clocks including hepatic and pancreatic metabolic clocks.
  • Eliminate Blue Light After Sunset: Blue light after dark suppresses melatonin and delays circadian phase. Melatonin is not just a sleep hormone — it’s an insulin sensitizer and antioxidant. Blue-blocking glasses, orange bulbs, dim lights in evening.
  • Room Temperature: 65–68°F (18–20°C) optimal for deep sleep. Cooler environments enhance GH secretion and BAT activation overnight.
  • Don’t Eat Within 3 Hours of Sleep: Late-night eating disrupts GH secretion (blocked by insulin), drives visceral fat storage, and misaligns hepatic circadian rhythms.
  • Sleep Apnea Screen: Obstructive sleep apnea is epidemic in metabolic syndrome and drives cortisol, insulin resistance, and leptin resistance in a vicious cycle. Any loud snoring + metabolic dysfunction warrants overnight oximetry or polysomnography. Treatment of OSA alone dramatically improves metabolic markers.
  • Magnesium Before Bed: 400–600mg magnesium glycinate or threonate improves GABA activity, deepens slow-wave sleep, and has direct insulin-sensitizing effects.
#4
Spirit & Purpose
STRESS PHYSIOLOGY & MEANING

Cortisol is the most underappreciated metabolic hormone. Chronic psychological stress produces sustained cortisol elevation — which directly causes hepatic glucose production, visceral fat deposition, insulin resistance, and muscle catabolism. Purpose-driven living measurably lowers cortisol baselines. Whether that’s God, your higher self, a deep belief in yourself, or simply what gets you out of bed before the alarm — this is your animating force, and it is profoundly metabolic.

  • Cortisol & Visceral Fat: Cortisol preferentially deposits fat in the visceral compartment (around organs) because visceral fat has 4× more glucocorticoid receptors than subcutaneous fat. Stress = belly fat. This is not metaphor, it is endocrinology.
  • Cortisol & Blood Sugar: Cortisol raises blood glucose by stimulating gluconeogenesis in the liver. The “stress hyperglycemia” phenomenon is real — CGM users regularly see glucose spike during an argument, traffic, or a stressful work meeting without eating anything.
  • HPA Axis Reset: Adaptogens (ashwagandha KSM-66, rhodiola, holy basil), breathwork, nature exposure, and mindfulness practice all measurably lower morning cortisol within 4–8 weeks. Choose what resonates — the best practice is the one you’ll actually do.
  • Purpose as Physiology: Ikigai research (Alimujiang et al., JAMA 2019) found people with strong sense of purpose have significantly lower all-cause mortality — and lower cortisol baselines. Purpose isn’t soft. It’s measurable biology.
  • Mindfulness & Insulin: An 8-week MBSR program reduced HbA1c by 0.5–0.8% in diabetic patients — comparable to some glucose-lowering medications (Hartmann et al., Diab Res Clin Prac 2012).
#5
Air
BREATHWORK & OXYGEN DELIVERY

Most people in metabolic distress are chronic over-breathers — mouth breathing, chest breathing, hyperventilating subtly — which keeps the nervous system locked in sympathetic dominance, impairs CO2 tolerance (needed for oxygen delivery to tissues via the Bohr effect), and elevates cortisol. Nasal breathing is the fastest, most accessible intervention to shift metabolic state within minutes. Oxygen delivery efficiency is directly linked to mitochondrial function.

  • Nasal Breathing Only: Nasal breathing produces nitric oxide (vasodilator, antimicrobial) and improves CO2 tolerance. Tape lips during sleep if mouth breathing. Apply during Zone 2 exercise — if you can’t maintain nasal breathing, you’re in Zone 3, slow down.
  • Box Breathing (4-4-4-4): 4 sec inhale, 4 sec hold, 4 sec exhale, 4 sec hold. 5 min before meals activates parasympathetic state, optimizing digestive enzyme secretion and nutrient absorption. Pre-meal breathwork is an underutilized metabolic tool.
  • CO2 Tolerance Training: Breath-hold after normal exhale. Work up to 20+ seconds. Longer breath-holds = better CO2 tolerance = better oxygen delivery to tissues = better mitochondrial function. Wim Hof, Buteyko, Oxygen Advantage all address this.
  • Physiological Sigh: Double inhale through nose then long exhale through mouth. The fastest single intervention to down-regulate the sympathetic nervous system (Stanford 2022, Balban et al., Cell Reports Medicine). Use during any stress response or before meals.
  • Indoor Air Quality: High indoor CO2 (>1000ppm, common in closed rooms) impairs cognitive function and may affect metabolic signaling. Air filtration, ventilation, houseplants (small effect but real).

Electrical
MITOCHONDRIA, LIGHT & GROUNDING

Mitochondrial function IS metabolic health. Every ATP molecule that powers insulin signaling, glucose uptake, fat oxidation, and thermogenesis is produced by mitochondria. Light, grounding, PEMF, and red light therapy are direct inputs to mitochondrial efficiency. This pillar is the one conventional medicine has not yet caught up to — and it may be the most underutilized lever in metabolic medicine.

  • Red & Near-Infrared Light Therapy (Photobiomodulation): 660nm (red) + 850nm (NIR) wavelengths stimulate cytochrome c oxidase in Complex IV of the electron transport chain, directly increasing ATP production and reducing oxidative stress. 10–20 min/day on abdomen and back. Studies show improvements in insulin sensitivity, adipose reduction, and thyroid function. (Avci et al., Semin Cutan Med Surg 2013)
  • Grounding / Earthing: Direct skin contact with earth (grass, soil, sand, water) transfers free electrons that act as antioxidants, reducing inflammatory markers (CRP, IL-6, TNF-α). 20–30 min barefoot outdoors daily. Grounding has been shown to improve cortisol rhythms, reduce pain, and improve sleep quality (Chevalier et al., J Environ Public Health 2012).
  • PEMF (Pulsed Electromagnetic Field): Enhances cellular energy production, improves cell membrane permeability and ion transport. Emerging evidence for metabolic benefits — PEMF improves mitochondrial function and has anti-inflammatory effects at the cellular level.
  • EMF Awareness: Chronic low-level EMF exposure may impair voltage-gated calcium channels and mitochondrial function. Practical mitigation: phone on airplane mode at night, no devices in bedroom, wired ethernet over WiFi where possible.
  • Morning Sunlight for Mitochondria: UV-A in morning sunlight directly activates melanopsin in skin, stimulating nitric oxide release and mitochondrial activation. The sun is the original photobiomodulation device.
  • Sauna (Infrared or Traditional): 3–4 sessions/week, 20–30 min at 170–200°F. Sauna use mimics moderate-intensity aerobic exercise, induces heat shock proteins, reduces all-cause mortality by 40% at 4+ sessions/week (Laukkanen et al., JAMA IM 2015), and improves insulin sensitivity independently.
#7
Water
HYDRATION & MINERAL BALANCE

Mild dehydration (just 1–2% body water deficit) measurably impairs metabolic rate, cognitive function, and physical performance. Water is a substrate for enzymatic reactions throughout metabolism. But not all water is equal — mineral content, structure, and source matter. Thirst is a late dehydration signal; by the time you’re thirsty, you’re already impaired.

  • Target 0.5 oz per lb body weight: Adjust up 16 oz for every hour of exercise or significant sweating. Sip consistently throughout the day rather than large boluses that flush through.
  • Morning Hydration Protocol: 16–32 oz of water with a pinch of sea salt and lemon juice immediately upon waking. Rehydrates after 8 hours of respiratory water loss, activates bowel motility, and provides electrolytes before coffee (which is diuretic).
  • Mineral-Rich Water: Reverse osmosis (RO) removes everything — reintroduce minerals with trace mineral drops or choose mineral-rich water (Gerolsteiner, Pellegrino, Mountain Valley Spring). Magnesium in water is more bioavailable than many supplement forms.
  • Structured Water: The fourth phase of water (EZ water, gel water) exists naturally in fruits, vegetables, and spring water. This intracellular water is critical for mitochondrial function and cellular signaling. Eat hydrating whole foods — cucumber, celery, berries, leafy greens — which provide structured water along with fiber and micronutrients.
  • Electrolyte Balance: Sodium, potassium, magnesium, chloride. Especially critical during low-carbohydrate or ketogenic phases (glycogen loss pulls water and electrolytes). Supplementation of sodium (1–2g), magnesium (400mg), and potassium (1–3g from food) prevents “keto flu” and supports metabolic adaptation.
  • Avoid Plastic Water Bottles: BPA and phthalates from plastics are endocrine disruptors that directly impair insulin signaling and thyroid function. Use glass, stainless steel, or copper vessels.
