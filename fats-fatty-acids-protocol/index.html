◆ The Assumption Worth Questioning

“Some fats are good for you and some fats are bad for you.”

Almost every fat on the “bad” list behaves differently depending on the food it arrives in, how it was processed, and the genetics of the person eating it. The same grams of dairy fat raise cholesterol measurably less inside cheese than inside butter. One of the four main saturated fats does not raise LDL at all. And the largest omega-3 trial ever run reported a null primary endpoint — while total heart attacks fell significantly, and a further signal appeared only once participants were separated by ancestry. So the question was never which fats are good. It is: what is this fat, in this food, doing in this body?

◆ THE CORE REFRAME

Fat is not fuel. Fat is information.

Fatty acids become the physical structure of your cell membranes, which changes how your receptors sit and signal — including the insulin receptor. They bind nuclear receptors and directly alter gene transcription. They are converted into resolvins and protectins, the molecules that actively switch inflammation off rather than merely failing to switch it on. Stearic acid attaches to proteins and reshapes mitochondria. Which is why “how much fat” was always the wrong question, and why a protocol about fat is really a protocol about signalling.

Fat As Information, Not Fuel

This is the idea the whole protocol is built on, so it is worth slowing down for. A calorie framing treats fat as something you burn. That is real, and it is the least interesting thing fat does.

It becomes you

Fatty acids are not consumed — they are incorporated. They become the physical membrane of every cell you have. Membrane composition changes how receptors sit inside that membrane, how readily they signal, and how the cell reads what is arriving at its surface. Change the fat you eat and, over roughly 120 days, you change the material your cells are built from. The insulin receptor sits in that membrane too.

It talks to your genes

You are born with the genes you have. What changes is which ones are switched on, how strongly, and when — and dietary fat is one of the inputs making that call. Omega-3s bind nuclear receptors and directly alter transcription. Stearic acid attaches to proteins and reshapes mitochondria. The food is not just substrate arriving; it is instruction arriving.

It becomes the signals

Your body converts these fats into resolvins and protectins — the molecules that actively switch inflammation off, rather than merely failing to switch it on. Ending inflammation is an active process that requires raw material. Without the substrate, inflammation does not resolve. It simply continues.

◆ Why Isolating A Nutrient Loses The Message

Take the carrot. It is rich in beta-carotene, and beta-carotene is genuinely good for you — so we extract it, put it in a capsule, and call that the active ingredient. But a carrot contains hundreds of other compounds, and we have studied almost none of them. Pulling one molecule out does not concentrate the message. It deletes most of it and keeps the loudest word. This is the same error as reducing a fat to its saturation percentage, and the same error as reducing butter to grams of saturated fat while ignoring what it arrived inside.

◆ AND THE STATE YOU EAT IN IS PART OF THE MESSAGE

Eating with other people, unhurried, sitting down, is what shifts you into parasympathetic dominance — and digestion is a parasympathetic function. Bile release and pancreatic enzyme secretion both run under vagal influence. So the identical meal, eaten braced and standing in a kitchen versus eaten at a table with people, is not the same physiological event. Your gut microbes are living in whichever of those two environments you hand them. You cannot digest a meal you are bracing against.

#1
Food

This is the pillar the whole protocol lives inside. Fat is not one decision but four: which fats you eat, what food they arrive in, how they were processed, and what you cook them in. The mechanism that matters most is the one nobody names — the food matrix. Calcium, phosphate and fat locked inside a protein structure form insoluble fatty acid soaps that are excreted rather than absorbed, which is why identical grams of dairy fat raise LDL measurably less inside cheese than inside butter. The second mechanism is oxidative load: polyunsaturated fats have more double bonds by definition, and every double bond is a site that can be attacked.

◆ PROTOCOL-SPECIFIC ACTION
Cook in extra virgin olive oil, ghee or tallow. Buy fats in packages that protect them — dark glass, small bottles, cold-pressed. And eat fat inside whole food more often than you eat it as an extracted oil.

The four questions, in order

1. What food is it in? Fermented dairy (cheese, yogurt) behaves better than butter, which behaves better than processed meat. Whole nuts behave better than nut oils. Whole fish behaves better than fish oil capsules. The matrix is doing work that the fatty acid label cannot show you.

2. How was it processed? Cold-pressed and unrefined preserves polyphenols and vitamin E — which is not incidental, because those are the antioxidants that protect the fat. Industrial refining strips them out by design.

3. What are you cooking it in? See the Fats A–Z tab. Smoke point is the wrong metric; oxidative stability is the right one, and they do not track together.

4. What is your background diet doing? Omega-3 and omega-6 compete for the same desaturase and elongase enzymes. A very high linoleic acid intake crowds out the omega-3 side regardless of how much fish you eat.

Fibre belongs in this pillar too

Viscous fibre — psyllium, acacia, beta-glucan, chia, ground flax — binds bile acids and forces the liver to upregulate LDL receptors. That is the same mechanism a statin uses, arrived at from the other direction. Target 25–40 g/day of total fibre, built up slowly. Tier 1

But fibre is a therapeutic with responders, not a universal requirement. In idiopathic constipation, reducing fibre has been shown to reduce symptoms — the opposite of standard advice — and insoluble fibre can worsen IBS. If fibre makes you worse, that is information, not failure.

Protein and the fat question

Adequate protein spares you from having to solve the fat-versus-carbohydrate argument at all. 1.2–1.6 g/kg for most adults, higher with resistance training or over 65.

#2
Movement

Movement changes fat metabolism through three separate mechanisms rather than one. It improves insulin sensitivity, which changes how much fat your liver packages into VLDL. It generates shear stress across the arterial tree, which switches on the endothelial protective program. And skeletal muscle contraction drives lymphatic flow, which is the actual route cholesterol takes out of the artery wall. Prolonged sitting drops leg endothelial function within hours, and a morning workout does not fully offset a sedentary day.

◆ PROTOCOL-SPECIFIC ACTION
Interrupt sitting every 30–45 minutes — two minutes is enough. Add Zone 2 cardio 150+ min/week (e.g., brisk walk where you can hold a full conversation, easy cycling, light swimming, conversational-pace jog) and resistance training twice weekly.

Zone 2 — the base

150–180 minutes weekly, in blocks of 30–60 minutes. This is the intensity that builds mitochondrial density and fat oxidation capacity. If you cannot speak in full sentences, you are above Zone 2 and getting a different (also useful) adaptation.

Resistance — the non-negotiable after 50

Twice weekly minimum, compound movements. Muscle is the largest glucose disposal site in the body; losing it is the fastest route to the metabolic picture that makes every fat question harder to answer.

The sitting problem specifically

This is separate from whether you exercise. Endothelial function in the legs measurably declines within 1–3 hours of uninterrupted sitting. Five air squats, a walk to the kitchen, or standing for two minutes restores it. Set a timer.

Post-meal movement

A ten to fifteen minute walk after the largest meal of the day blunts the postprandial glucose and triglyceride rise more than the same walk at another time. Highest-yield ten minutes in the day for anyone with a metabolic question.

#3
Sleep & Light

One week of short sleep measurably worsens insulin sensitivity in healthy young adults — which changes fat handling directly, because insulin is one of the modulators of the desaturase enzymes that build your long-chain fatty acids. Genova’s own reference material lists insulin alongside zinc and the B vitamins as a regulator of that pathway. And untreated sleep apnea is the most commonly missed reversible cause of a bad vascular picture — it drives intermittent hypoxia, sympathetic activation and oxidative stress simultaneously.

◆ PROTOCOL-SPECIFIC ACTION
If you snore, wake unrefreshed, get morning headaches, or have blood pressure that will not come down — pull that thread before adjusting anything else in this protocol.

The apnea question

Home sleep testing has become good enough that there is little excuse not to look. The classic picture is a snoring, tired, hypertensive man — but it is substantially underdiagnosed in women, where it presents more often as insomnia, fatigue and mood change rather than snoring.

Light as a metabolic signal

Morning light within an hour of waking anchors circadian timing, which anchors the timing of insulin sensitivity across the day. Evening blue light does the opposite. This is not soft advice — melatonin and insulin sensitivity are linked, and eating late in one’s biological night produces a worse metabolic response to the identical meal.

Eating window

For most people the highest-yield version of time-restricted eating is simply finish earlier, not start later. A 12-hour overnight fast that ends at 7pm beats a compressed window that ends at 10pm.

#4
Water

Fat digestion is not a water-independent process. Bile is an aqueous secretion, and adequate hydration supports both bile flow and lymphatic transport — the route by which cholesterol actually leaves the artery wall. Mineral content matters as much as volume: magnesium is a required cofactor for the desaturase enzymes that build long-chain fatty acids, and it is one of the most commonly low minerals in people eating a modern diet. Reverse-osmosis water with nothing added back is a slow way to lose ground.

Practical: filtered water with minerals restored, or a pinch of unrefined salt and a magnesium supplement taken separately. Aim for pale straw urine rather than a fixed number of glasses — requirements vary enormously with body size, activity, climate and caffeine intake.

Alcohol belongs in this pillar as much as anywhere. It is the single most underdiscussed driver of triglycerides and fatty liver in adults over 50, and Genova’s reference material lists it explicitly among the influences on fatty acid desaturase enzymes. If triglycerides are high and the cause is not obvious, look here before looking anywhere clever.

#5
Air

Nasal breathing generates nitric oxide from the paranasal sinuses, and nitric oxide is the endothelial molecule that the whole flow-and-plaque story runs on. Deep diaphragmatic breathing also drives the thoracic duct — the main lymphatic return — through pressure changes, which matters because lymphatic vessels are the exit route for cholesterol from the artery wall and have no central pump driving them. Air quality matters too: fine particulate exposure is an independent oxidative and cardiovascular stressor.

Practical: nasal breathing as the default, including during Zone 2 work. Five minutes of slow diaphragmatic breathing daily — longer exhale than inhale. A HEPA filter in the bedroom if you live near traffic.

Note the honest tier here. That deep breathing moves lymph is well established. That it measurably changes cholesterol clearance in humans is Tier 3 — a mechanistically reasonable idea that nobody has tested.

#6
Spirit & Purpose

Whether that’s God, your higher self, a deep belief in yourself, or simply what gets you out of bed before the alarm — this is your animating force. It belongs in a protocol about fat for a concrete reason: dietary change that is not connected to something a person actually cares about does not survive contact with a difficult month. Every camp in the diet wars has adherents who thrive, and the common factor is rarely the macronutrient ratio. It is that the way of eating means something to them.

The most rigorous comparison of low-fat versus low-carbohydrate diets found no difference in weight change at twelve months, and found that neither genotype nor insulin secretion predicted who did better. What did predict success in the secondary analyses was adherence and diet quality. Purpose is upstream of adherence.

#7
Electrical

Mitochondria are where fatty acids are actually burned, and mitochondrial function is measurably influenced by light exposure, grounding and electromagnetic environment. The most concrete link in this protocol: stearic acid drives mitochondrial fusion through a process called stearoylation, raising oxygen consumption and fat oxidation after a meal. That is a dietary fat acting directly on mitochondrial architecture. Tier 2

Red and near-infrared photobiomodulation has plausible mechanism via cytochrome c oxidase and some human data on local outcomes; systemic metabolic claims remain Tier 3.

#8
Community & Connection

The most underestimated pillar, and in this protocol it has a specific physiological argument rather than a sentimental one. Digestion is a parasympathetic function. Fat digestion in particular requires bile release and pancreatic lipase, both under vagal influence. Eating in a sympathetically dominant state — rushed, standing, stressed, alone in a car — is not the same physiological act as eating the identical meal at a table with people. The Targets, With The Lab’s Ranges Beside Them

Average is not normal, and normal is not optimal. A laboratory reference range describes the middle of a population in which chronic disease is ordinary. One major fatty acid panel states this outright in its own fine print: “The Essential Fatty Acid reference ranges are based on an adult population.” An adult population — not a healthy one.
◆ TWO THINGS TO UNDERSTAND BEFORE READING ANY FATTY ACID PANEL

1. Red blood cell membrane, not whole blood. RBC membrane reflects roughly 120 days of tissue incorporation. Whole blood is meaningfully moved by what you ate yesterday — a salmon dinner can shift a whole-blood result and cannot shift an RBC one. Order the RBC version specifically. Ask by name, and check which matrix your lab actually runs — several large conventional labs report a whole-blood fatty acid panel by default, and the result will not mean what you think it means. If whole blood is genuinely all you can get, it is not useless; just read it knowing it reflects recent intake rather than months of tissue status. Tier 1

2. Some of these ranges are one-tailed, and that is deliberate. The omega-3 markers are reported as a floor (≥) rather than a range, because — in the lab’s own words — “the reference ranges for omega-3 fatty acids are one-tailed since their health benefits are well-studied and deficiencies are associated with many clinical conditions.” There is no established upper limit for most of them.

A panel can report % Omega-3s of 4.9 against a floor of 3.8 — passing — while the Omega-3 Index on the same page reads 3.1 against 4.0, in the high-risk zone. Both numbers are correct. ◆ Female-specific · why LDL commonly rises at menopause

Oestrogen upregulates hepatic LDL receptors — the mechanism by which the liver clears LDL particles out of circulation. As oestrogen falls through perimenopause and menopause, that clearance capacity falls with it, and LDL and ApoB commonly rise without any change in diet at all. This is one of the most frequent and most misattributed lab findings in women over 45: the number moves, the woman is told she has started eating badly, and the actual driver is hormonal. It does not mean the rise is harmless — it means the cause has been identified rather than guessed at, and hormonal status belongs in the workup alongside the lipid panel. Tier 2

Scenario 1 · The commonest one
My Omega-3 Index is low. Now what?
The answer depends entirely on why it is low, and there are four distinct reasons that look identical on the report.
  • Intake. Are you actually eating oily fish twice a week, or supplementing? Most low results are simply this. Marine EPA + DHA is the direct route; plant ALA is not equivalent (see Scenario 2).
  • Form and absorption. If you supplement and the number has not moved, check the form. Ethyl ester absorbs substantially less well than re-esterified triglyceride and is worse on an empty stomach. Take it with a fat-containing meal.
  • Conversion. Look at the delta-6-desaturase readout on the same panel. If it is high, substrate is accumulating because the enzyme is not moving it.
  • Cofactors. Zinc, B6, B3, magnesium and vitamin C are all required by these enzymes. Genetics loads the gun; missing cofactors can stall the pathway regardless.
  • Competition. A very high linoleic acid intake crowds out the omega-3 side for the same enzymes. Reducing refined oil intake can raise the Index without changing fish intake at all.
Scenario 2 · Sex-specific
I eat ground flax daily. Am I covered?
Probably not, and it depends on your sex and hormonal stage. Conversion is poor, and the useful numbers are sex-specific rather than general: in men, roughly 8% of ALA reaches EPA and DHA formation is not reliably detectable; in women of reproductive age, roughly 21% reaches EPA and about 9% reaches DHA.
  • Men: flax is fibre, lignans and ALA — all genuinely useful, none of it a reliable DHA source. If you do not eat fish, algae oil delivers DHA directly and skips the conversion step.
  • Women of reproductive age: conversion is meaningfully better, because oestrogen upregulates the desaturase and elongase enzymes.
  • Postmenopausal women: the advantage is oestrogen-driven, so expect it to narrow. Reference material specifies women of reproductive age for the enhanced conversion. Read the men’s column until your own Index says otherwise.
  • In all three cases the resolution is the same: measure, do not assume.
Scenario 3 · The one that causes arguments
My LDL went UP on a low-carbohydrate diet. Should I be worried?
This is a recognised phenotype and it is genuinely unsettled. Lean, metabolically healthy people with low triglycerides and high HDL can develop markedly high LDL on carbohydrate restriction.
  • What is agreed: the phenotype is real, reproducible, and not the same population the original lipid trials studied.
  • What one camp argued: a one-year imaging study of this group reported that existing plaque predicted progression while ApoB did not. That paper was retracted in May 2026, at the request of both the authors and the editors, for methodological errors described as too great to correct. Three of the authors state they did not have access to the data before publication and alerted the journal themselves; a coauthor was an executive at the imaging company. Treat any argument still resting on it accordingly.
  • What the critics argued, before the retraction: the prespecified primary endpoint — soft plaque volume — rose substantially over the year, there was no control group, and everyone enrolled already had high ApoB, which makes it statistically very hard to detect an ApoB relationship inside such a narrow range. So the honest position today is that we know less about this phenotype than we appeared to in early 2025.
  • The reasonable move: stop arguing from first principles and look. Imaging converts this from a debate into a measurement. See the Lipid & Vascular protocol for which scan answers which question.
  • What not to do: assume it is fine because you feel well, and equally, do not panic-treat a number without knowing whether there is any plaque.
Scenario 4 · Digestive
I feel unwell after fatty meals.
Before adjusting fat intake, work out which step is failing. Fat digestion has three distinct requirements and they fail differently.
  • Bile. Gallbladder removed, sluggish flow, or bile acid insufficiency — pale stools, floating stools, right upper discomfort. Options a physician might consider here include pre-meal bitters and, in some cases, supplemental bile acids — both decisions that belong with your own clinician rather than with a protocol.
  • Pancreatic lipase. Undigested fat, urgency, visible oil. Worth formal assessment rather than guesswork.
  • Nervous system state. Digestion is parasympathetic. Eating rushed, standing or stressed genuinely impairs fat digestion. Sit down, three slow breaths, then eat — before concluding you cannot tolerate fat.
  • Then reintroduce gradually rather than eliminating fat, because fat-soluble vitamin status depends on it.
Scenario 5 · Genetic
My genetics say I am a poor converter. Does that settle it?
No. A variant tells you where the system is structurally vulnerable so the protocol can defend that specific weakness. Genetics loads the gun. Lifestyle and a personalised protocol decide whether the trigger ever gets pulled.
  • Match the marker to the exposure. FADS variants predict how well you convert plant ALA into EPA and DHA. They do not predict your response to saturated fat, or to carbohydrate. Conflating them is a common and consequential error.
  • Poor converter is not a sentence — it is a routing instruction. It means get EPA and DHA preformed, from marine or algal sources, rather than relying on conversion.
  • Check the cofactors anyway. Enzyme capacity and cofactor supply are separate failure points and both are addressable.
  • Then measure. The Omega-3 Index is the phenotype. The genotype is a hypothesis about it.
Scenario 6 · Practical
I want to change one thing this week. What is the highest yield?
For most people, the cooking fat — because it is daily, it is cheap, and it is the input most likely to be quietly oxidised.
  • Switch everyday cooking to extra virgin olive oil; use ghee or tallow for genuinely high heat.
  • Buy in dark glass, in a size you will finish within a couple of months, and store away from the stove.
  • If you are already doing that, the next lever is an Omega-3 Index test — because everything after this point depends on knowing where you actually sit.

Fats A–Z

◆ Start Here: Smoke Point Is The Wrong Number

The number on the bottle tells you when an oil visibly smokes. It does not tell you when it starts breaking down.

When commercial oils were actually heated and their breakdown products measured, extra virgin olive oil sat at the top end of oxidative stability, ahead of several oils with substantially higher smoke points — sunflower, grapeseed, rice bran and canola among them. The conclusion was that initial smoke point correlates poorly with an oil’s stability under cooking heat or with polar compound formation. Source caveat: that comparison was run by a laboratory owned by the olive industry and published in a low-tier journal, so treat the league table cautiously and the mechanism confidently. Tier 2

Relative oxidative stability under cooking heat, ordered by direction of finding rather than by measured value. Two drivers explain the whole ranking: how many double bonds are present to be attacked, and whether the oil’s own antioxidants are still in it. Refining strips the second. How To Tell If Your Olive Oil Is Actually Any Good

Taste it neat. You are looking for a peppery burn at the back of the throat — a catch that can make you cough. That sensation is oleocanthal and the related polyphenols, and it is the closest thing to a home assay for the compounds that make olive oil stable and useful.

And it is a working scale, not a pass/fail. The stronger the burn, the more polyphenol protection is present, and the more confidently you can cook with it at higher temperatures. A mild, buttery, pleasant oil is telling you something real: most of that protection is gone.

Price does not predict this. Three of the most expensive bottles on a good grocery store shelf, bought and tasted side by side, delivered almost no pepper at all. You are frequently paying for packaging, provenance stories and shelf position — none of which correlate with what your tongue can measure in two seconds.

Certification helps with one problem and not the other. A purity certification makes it more likely the oil has not been cut with cheaper oils. There is no certification anywhere for actual antioxidant potential — which is the property that made olive oil worth buying in the first place. Freshness matters too: harvest date beats "best before," and light and heat degrade polyphenols, so dark glass stored away from the stove is not fussiness.

What Industrial Refining Actually Does

1. Extraction. Seeds are pressed, then washed with hexane — a petroleum solvent — to recover the remaining oil.

2. Degumming and bleaching. Phospholipids and pigments removed.

3. Deodorisation. Steam at 180–240°C strips odour compounds. Above roughly 240°C this step itself generates trans fatty acids and α,β-unsaturated aldehydes. There is a genuine engineering tension here: lower temperatures reduce trans fat formation but leave objectionable volatiles behind.

Left to right: what went in, what we do to it, and what comes out. Between the seed and the bottle sit hexane extraction, degumming, bleaching, and steam deodorisation at 180–240°C. The molecule that leaves the refinery is not the molecule that was in the seed — which is why a study measuring “linoleic acid” may be measuring two quite different exposures depending on how the oil was made.

4. Then it is heated again in your kitchen. The molecule that leaves the refinery is not the molecule that was in the seed, and the molecule in your pan after twenty minutes is not the one that left the refinery. Tier 2

Saturated Fat — The Six Studies Most Often Quoted, On Both Sides

Six serious studies. They do not agree. Anyone quoting you only one column is selling something.

Strongest for cutting saturated fat

Cochrane 2020. 15 randomised trials, 56,675 participants, minimum 2 years, and it deliberately excluded multifactorial trials so no diet-plus-exercise-plus-meditation confounding. Result: 17% fewer combined cardiovascular events (RR 0.83, 0.70–0.98), with a dose-response — the more saturated fat was cut, the greater the reduction.

LA Veterans Diet Study, 1969. Double-blind, with the institution controlling the actual food rather than giving advice, which is very hard to do. Reduced atherosclerotic events. Limitation: roughly 400 men per arm, men only, average age around 65.

Mendelian randomisation on ApoB. Not a diet trial at all — it uses inherited genetic variation as a natural experiment. It is the strongest causal evidence that the ApoB particle itself drives disease, and it sidesteps every confounder in the “why food studies are hard” list. Saturated fat skeptics largely do not engage with it.

Strongest against the restriction advice

Minnesota Coronary Experiment. 9,570 participants, the largest dietary trial ever run, institutional feeding, with autopsy data. Cholesterol fell 13.8% and there was no mortality benefit — with a 22% higher death risk per 30 mg/dL of cholesterol lowering. The data sat unpublished for about 40 years.

Women’s Health Initiative Dietary Modification. Over 48,000 women, 8+ years of low-fat modification. Null for coronary heart disease — neither benefit nor harm.

Sydney Diet Heart Study. Recovered data showed higher coronary death in the intervention arm. Limitation: roughly 450 men, men only, aged 30–60, all with established coronary disease, and the replacement fat was omega-6-rich safflower oil specifically.

The Food Matrix — The Most Underused Idea Here

Identical grams of dairy fat produce a measurably different LDL response depending on the food they arrive in. In randomised crossover work, dairy fat eaten inside the cheese matrix raised LDL less than the same dairy fat eaten as butter.

The mechanism. In cheese, calcium, phosphate and fat are encapsulated together inside a protein structure. That proximity allows calcium and fatty acids to form insoluble fatty acid soaps, which are excreted rather than absorbed — and faecal fat excretion measurably rises on cheese and milk diets. Butter contains essentially no calcium, no protein matrix and less phosphate, so no soaps form.

In the outcome data, cheese is inversely associated with cardiovascular risk while butter is not. The food is the unit of analysis, not the fatty acid. Tier 2

On European-style butters. Irish and continental butters are typically churned to a higher butterfat and lower water content than standard commercial butter, which is why they behave differently in cooking and taste richer. And now the part that complicates it. A randomised controlled trial compared butter from mountain-pasture grazing cows against conventional Danish butter and found no differences in blood lipids, lipoproteins, hsCRP, insulin, glucose or glucose tolerance. What did move the numbers was the form, not the farm. In an eight-week randomised trial, 57 adults took 40 g of milk fat daily as either whipping cream — where the fat is still enclosed in its natural milk fat globule membrane — or as butter oil, where that membrane has been stripped away. This is the food matrix argument again, and it is the cleanest demonstration of it in the whole protocol: identical fat, identical dose, from the same animal — and the only variable was whether its membrane was intact. Ruminant Trans Fat Is Not Industrial Trans Fat

“Trans” describes a molecular shape, not a source. Two entirely different origins produce it.

Industrial: hydrogen forced into liquid vegetable oil under pressure with a metal catalyst to make it solid and shelf-stable. Predominantly elaidic acid. This is what raises LDL, lowers HDL, and was banned. Tier 1

Ruminant: bacteria in the rumen of cows, sheep and goats partially hydrogenating grasses as ordinary digestion. Produces vaccenic acid and cis-9, trans-11 CLA. Present in butter, cream and beef fat, and eaten by humans for as long as we have kept dairy animals.

The evidence separates them. Epidemiology associates coronary risk with industrial isomers and not ruminant ones, and a systematic review with meta-regression found no relationship between ruminant trans fat intake up to 4.19% of daily energy and the two blood lipid ratios it examined in healthy people. Read that precisely: the review measured cholesterol ratios only, its own authors caution against inferring cardiovascular risk from lipid markers alone, and 4.19% was the highest dose in the pooled trials rather than a validated ceiling — real intake runs nearer 0.5–1%. The first author was affiliated with a dairy industry body. What it does support is that the two isomers are not interchangeable, which blanket labelling ignores. Tier 2

Constitutional And Typing Systems

Several traditions arrive independently at the same intuition: that people differ constitutionally in what suits them. That intuition is almost certainly correct. Whether any given system measures it correctly is a separate question, and the answers differ.

Ayurvedic Prakriti — Vata, Pitta, Kapha. Tier 2 In genome-wide work, 3,416 individuals were screened by Ayurvedic physicians blind to laboratory results, and 262 well-classified men were genotyped; 52 SNPs significantly discriminated the three constitutional types, and the classification held independent of ancestry. Small, male-only, and replication remains limited — but a real finding. That is real genomic structure underlying a classification system three millennia older than the genome. It does not validate every dietary rule built on top of it, and replication remains limited — but it is a great deal more than nothing.

Autonomic / metabolic typing. Tier 3 The claim is that sympathetic-dominant and parasympathetic-dominant people need different diets. The underlying physiology is not in dispute: digestion is parasympathetic, and autonomic balance genuinely influences digestion and metabolism. What is missing is controlled evidence that typing predicts dietary response. Note carefully: the one formally tested protocol from this lineage was a cancer treatment regimen, not the typing framework, and it performed markedly worse than chemotherapy. Those are separate claims and should not be conflated in either direction.

Blood-type diet. Tier 3 The most instructive negative result in this whole area. In 1,455 people with ABO determined by genotyping rather than self-report, adherence to the Type-A pattern was associated with lower BMI, blood pressure, cholesterol, triglycerides and insulin — and the association was entirely independent of the person’s actual blood type. The pattern tracked with better markers; blood type contributed nothing. Cross-sectional, so association rather than proven effect — but the genotype-independence conclusion is sound.

41% of calories from fat, cooked in lard

Aha Moments

Twelve questions with real citations. Most carry an explicit limitation; where one is absent, the claim is one where the evidence is not seriously contested. Volunteering the caveat inside the moment is what converts a surprising claim into a credible one.

Aha Moment 1 · Conventional medicine blind spot
The largest omega-3 trial ever run said it does not work. What if it failed because it averaged different people together?
A major randomised trial reported a null result on its primary composite endpoint — although total myocardial infarction was significantly reduced across the whole trial, which the headline lost. A secondary analysis stratified by self-reported ancestry found a notable reduction in myocardial infarction risk in African Americans. The proposed explanation is genetic: roughly 80% of African Americans carry two copies of the FADS “derived” haplotype associated with efficient fatty acid conversion, versus about 43% of non-Hispanic White individuals. The trial did not fail. The average did. Honest limitation: this is a secondary subgroup analysis, which is hypothesis-generating rather than confirmatory, and it needs prospective testing.
  • Citation: VITAL trial and its ancestry-stratified secondary analysis; FADS haplotype frequency data in African American and European American cohorts
  • Delivery tip: Ask the question. Pause five full seconds. Then answer.
  • Best placed: Opening hook, or immediately after any genetics section
Aha Moment 2 · Systems thinking
You take ground flax for the omega-3. If you are a man, how much of it becomes DHA?
None detectable. Conversion of ALA to DHA runs at roughly 9% in women of reproductive age and is not reliably detectable in men (ALA to EPA: about 21% versus 8%) — oestrogen upregulates the desaturase and elongase enzymes. Over six months on an ALA-rich diet, women gained about 2.0% EPA in plasma phospholipids; men gained 0.7%. Flaxseed is not fish oil, and for men it is barely omega-3 in the sense that matters. Honest limitation: flax remains genuinely valuable for fibre and lignans; this is a statement about DHA specifically.
  • Citation: Burdge and Wootton; Childs and colleagues, on sex-specific ALA conversion
  • Delivery tip: Ask for a guess out loud before answering. Nobody guesses zero.
  • Best placed: After any omega-3 source discussion
Aha Moment 3 · Conventional medicine blind spot
You switched to avocado oil to avoid seed oils. What are the odds you actually did?
In United States testing, at least 82% of commercial avocado oil samples were rancid before their expiry date or adulterated with cheaper oils. A follow-up found 70% of private-label products the same, and only two brands in the tested set came back fully pure. There is no enforceable purity standard. Honest limitation: this is a market-integrity problem, not a fat-chemistry problem — genuine avocado oil is a perfectly reasonable fat.
  • Citation: UC Davis Olive Center avocado oil quality and purity studies, 2020 and follow-ups
  • Delivery tip: Let them guess the percentage first.
  • Best placed: Cooking fats section
Aha Moment 4 · Timeline / causality
Same grams of dairy fat. Why does cheese raise your cholesterol less than butter?
In cheese, calcium, phosphate and fat are encapsulated together inside a protein structure. That proximity lets calcium and fatty acids form insoluble soaps which are excreted rather than absorbed — and faecal fat excretion measurably rises on cheese and milk diets but not butter. Randomised crossover work confirms the LDL difference. The food is the unit of analysis, not the fatty acid. Honest limitation: the effect size is meaningful but not enormous; this reframes the question rather than licensing unlimited cheese.
  • Citation: Dairy matrix randomised controlled trials; dairy calcium and faecal fat excretion studies
  • Delivery tip: Ask it as a genuine puzzle. Most clinicians do not know this.
  • Best placed: Anywhere saturated fat is discussed
Aha Moment 5 · Organism / microbiology
Your fish is a health food. Is it the same fish it was twenty years ago?
Across a decade of feed reformulation, long-chain omega-3 in farmed Atlantic salmon fell by roughly half (Scottish data, 2006–2015), and in separate Tasmanian data the omega-3 to omega-6 ratio fell from about 8:1 to below 1:1 — driven by substituting vegetable oils for fish oil in feed. In fairness: farmed salmon still delivers more EPA and DHA per serving than most other fish and all terrestrial livestock. Wild fish run about 1% linoleic acid; farmed can run 9–10%, effectively a fingerprint for vegetable oil in the diet. The fish did not change. What we fed it did.
  • Citation: Sprague, Dick and Tocher, Scientific Reports, 2016 (Scottish); Nichols and colleagues, Nutrients, 2014 (Tasmanian)
  • Delivery tip: Works well immediately before any “just eat more fish” recommendation.
  • Best placed: Omega-3 sources section
Aha Moment 6 · Conventional medicine blind spot
Both camps quote the same review on saturated fat. How can both be telling the truth?
A systematic review of 15 randomised trials in 56,675 people — which deliberately excluded multifactorial trials — found a 17% reduction in combined cardiovascular events with a dose-response relationship. The same review found no reduction in all-cause mortality, cardiovascular mortality, non-fatal myocardial infarction, or coronary heart disease mortality, with a number needed to treat of 56 people for four years to prevent one event. Both camps quote it accurately. This is the composite endpoint problem, live.
  • Citation: Hooper and colleagues, Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 2020
  • Delivery tip: Walk one column completely before touching the other. Do not lean.
  • Best placed: The centre of any saturated fat discussion
Aha Moment 7 · Systems thinking
Smoke point tells you when an oil smokes. Does it tell you when the oil starts breaking down?
No, and they barely correlate. When commercial oils were heated and their breakdown products measured, extra virgin olive oil sat at the top end of oxidative stability, ahead of several oils with far higher smoke points. Two things drive stability: how many double bonds are present to attack, and whether the oil’s own polyphenols survived refining. Honest limitation: a comparison of heated commercial oils rather than a clinical outcome study — and it was run by an olive-industry laboratory in a low-tier journal, which you should say out loud before anyone finds it.
  • Citation: De Alzaa, Guillaume and Ravetti, Acta Scientific Nutritional Health, 2018
  • Delivery tip: Ask who was taught to buy high-smoke-point oil for cooking. Most hands go up.
  • Best placed: Cooking fats section
Aha Moment 8 · Brain-body connection
One of the four main saturated fats does not raise LDL at all. Which one, and what does it do instead?
Stearic acid (C18:0) — the dominant saturated fat in beef tallow and cocoa butter. Beyond being LDL-neutral, dietary stearic acid promotes mitochondrial fusion and raises fat oxidation after a meal in humans. The upstream mechanism — stearoylation of proteins and reduced JNK activation — comes from earlier fly and cell work rather than the human study. Honest limitation: the human data is short-term mechanistic work, the molecular mechanism is preclinical, and there are no long-term outcome trials.
  • Citation: Senyilmaz-Tiebe and colleagues, Nature Communications, 2018 (human, mitochondrial fusion); upstream stearoylation mechanism from Senyilmaz and colleagues, Nature, 2015 (preclinical)
  • Delivery tip: Land on the mitochondrial finding, not the LDL point — that is the surprise.
  • Best placed: After the saturated fat breakdown
Aha Moment 9 · Timeline / causality
Two populations. One eats 41% fat and cooks in lard. The other eats 72% carbohydrate. Which one has heart disease?
Neither, meaningfully. An Italian-American town studied from 1955 to 1961 had less than half the myocardial infarction death rate of its neighbours despite obesity, smoking and lard — and rates rose to match once the three-generation households dissolved. A Bolivian forager-horticulturalist population showed 85% with zero coronary calcium, the lowest coronary disease ever recorded, on a very high carbohydrate diet with a heavy inflammatory burden. What they share is unprocessed food, constant movement, dense social structure and no industrial oils. Honest limitation: the first has methodological criticisms; the second is cross-sectional and differs on many axes beyond diet.
  • Citation: Roseto mortality studies, Wolf and colleagues; Kaplan and colleagues, The Lancet, 2017
  • Delivery tip: Present them as a pair. Neither camp can claim either one.
  • Best placed: After the diet camps
Aha Moment 10 · Systems thinking
Your genetics say you convert omega-3 efficiently. Is that enough?
No. The desaturase enzymes require B2, B3, B6, vitamin C, zinc and magnesium, and are modulated by insulin; the elongases require B3, B5, B6, biotin and vitamin C. A person can carry efficient-converter genetics, eat the fish, and still not build DHA if zinc or B6 is short. Alcohol, cortisol and adrenocorticoids also influence these enzymes — which quietly puts alcohol inside the fatty acid pathway itself, where almost nobody places it.
  • Citation: Fatty acid metabolism cofactor assignments, Genova Diagnostics reference material
  • Delivery tip: Use it to reframe genetics as a starting point rather than a verdict.
  • Best placed: Immediately after any FADS discussion
Aha Moment 11 · Conventional medicine blind spot
Your butter label lists trans fat. Is it the trans fat that was banned?
No. “Trans” is a molecular shape, not a source. Industrial trans fat — predominantly elaidic acid — comes from forcing hydrogen into vegetable oil, and that is what raises LDL, lowers HDL and was banned. Ruminant trans fat — vaccenic acid and cis-9,trans-11 CLA — is produced by bacteria in a cow’s rumen and has been eaten for as long as humans have kept dairy animals. A systematic review with meta-regression found no relationship between ruminant trans fat intake up to 4.19% of daily energy and the blood lipid ratios it examined. Blanket labelling swept both into one category. Honest limitation: that review measured cholesterol ratios only, its authors caution against inferring risk from lipids alone, and the first author was affiliated with a dairy industry body.
  • Citation: Systematic review and meta-regression of ruminant trans fatty acid dose and cardiovascular risk markers; comparative epidemiology of industrial versus ruminant isomers
  • Delivery tip: Hold up the concept of a butter label. It makes it concrete.
  • Best placed: Butter or saturated fat section
Aha Moment 12 · Brain-body connection
The blood type diet worked in the largest study of it. So why is it wrong?
In 1,455 people with ABO blood group determined by genotyping rather than self-report, following the Type-A dietary pattern was associated with lower BMI, waist circumference, blood pressure, cholesterol, triglycerides and insulin. And the benefit was entirely independent of the person’s actual blood type. The pattern was associated with better markers; the personalisation mechanism was not. Anyone eating that pattern showed the association. Honest limitation: this was cross-sectional in young adults using a food-frequency questionnaire, so it demonstrates association rather than effect — and the Type-A score is largely a plant-density score, which invites healthy-user confounding. The genotype-independence finding is the robust part. It is the cleanest demonstration in nutrition that an intervention working and the stated reason it works are separate claims that require separate evidence.
  • Citation: Wang and colleagues, PLOS ONE, 2014
  • Delivery tip: Pair it with any genotype-based dietary claim, including the ones in this protocol.
  • Best placed: After the typing systems section

Top 3 Failure Points

1. Reading % Omega-3s instead of the Omega-3 Index. The first can pass while the second sits in the high-risk zone on the same page. They are different measurements with different cutoffs. Read the Index.

2. Assuming plant ALA covers DHA. DHA conversion is about 9% in women of reproductive age and not reliably detectable in men. The people most confident they have this handled are frequently the ones who do not.

3. Optimising the fat and ignoring the cofactors. Zinc, magnesium and B6 are required by the enzymes that build every long-chain fatty acid in your body. A stalled pathway with excellent intake is usually this.
