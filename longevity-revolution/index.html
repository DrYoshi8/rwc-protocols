Chronic low-grade inflammation — “inflammaging” — is the thread that connects almost every age-related disease. Unlike the inflammation you feel from a sprained ankle, this fire burns silently for years or decades before symptoms appear. It ages your brain, your joints, your heart, and your metabolism simultaneously. It is not one disease. It is one signal wearing dozens of masks.

How This Pattern Works

  • NF-κB pathway activation → pro-inflammatory cytokine cascade (IL-6, TNF-α, IL-1β)
  • Senescent (“zombie”) cells accumulate and secrete SASP — a continuous inflammatory storm into surrounding tissue
  • Toll-like receptor (TLR) dysregulation → chronic innate immune overactivation
  • Gut permeability allows LPS endotoxin into the bloodstream → systemic inflammaging loop
  • COX-2 upregulation + prostaglandin E2 → cyclical inflammatory amplification

  • Poor recovery from exercise or illness

  • Omega-3 EPA+DHA (2–4g daily) — resolvin and protectin synthesis, NF-κB inhibition

Atrophic Pattern — “The Slow Disappearance”

Stem Cell Exhaustion Telomere Attrition Epigenetic Alterations Loss of Proteostasis

After 40, you begin losing muscle mass at 1–2% per year — more if sedentary, under-eating protein, or hormonally insufficient. By 70, the average person has lost 30–40% of their muscle mass. This is not cosmetic. Muscle is your metabolic engine, injury buffer, insulin reservoir, and longevity asset. Bone loss parallels muscle loss. This pattern is 100% addressable — but only if you know it is active.

How This Pattern Works

  • Stem cell exhaustion → failure of tissue renewal in muscle, bone, skin, and neural tissue
  • mTOR dysregulation → impaired protein synthesis + progressive anabolic resistance
  • IGF-1/GH axis decline → reduced regenerative signaling to peripheral tissues
  • Telomere attrition → replicative senescence in progenitor and satellite cells
  • Epigenetic silencing of regenerative gene programs (MyoD, Pax7, Sox2)
Do These Feel Familiar? (Pattern 2 Indicators)
  • Difficulty building or maintaining muscle despite working out
  • Strength declining year over year
  • Low or declining bone density (osteopenia/osteoporosis)
  • Feeling “soft” or “skinny-fat” despite regular exercise
  • Low libido and/or competitive drive
  • Protein intake under 100g/day for most adults
  • Fatigue with low mood, particularly in men with low testosterone

The Protein Reality

  • Minimum 1.1–1.6g protein per kg body weight daily — adjusted for activity, goals, and training volume
  • Prioritize leucine-rich sources: eggs, meat, fish, Greek yogurt, whey or collagen peptides
  • Spread protein across meals — cells have a max synthesis window per sitting (~30–50g)
  • Collagen peptides (15–20g) support joint matrix, skin, and gut lining in addition to muscle
⚡ Female-Specific: Dr. Stacy Sims Protocol

Women’s muscle physiology differs significantly from men’s — and most exercise and nutrition research was done on men. Key principles for women 35+:

• No fasted training — fasting disrupts kisspeptin neurons that regulate female hormonal signaling. Women have two kisspeptin neuron areas affected (men have one). Eat a small protein-rich snack (10–15g) before training.
• Fasted cardio burns muscle, not fat — in women, the metabolic response to fasted exercise defaults to amino acid catabolism. This is the opposite of the desired effect.
• 45-minute post-training protein window — women must consume 35–60g protein within 45 minutes of resistance training to maximize muscle protein synthesis. This window is tighter and more critical than for men.
• Heavy compound lifting is essential — women 35+ need 4–6 rep max compound movements (squats, deadlifts, pressing) 2–3x/week. This stimulates testosterone and growth hormone release that lighter weights cannot replicate.
• Perimenopause can begin at 35 — hormonal shifts affecting muscle, bone, and brain can begin more than a decade before the final menstrual period. Do not wait for period changes to investigate.

Exercise for Longevity

  • Resistance training 2–4x/week — non-negotiable for healthy aging regardless of gender
  • Zone 2 cardio 150+ min/week: brisk walk where you can hold a full conversation, easy cycling, light swimming
  • Avoid the “moderate intensity trap” — chronic moderate-intensity cardio elevates cortisol without compensatory GH/testosterone surge
  • Brief high-intensity intervals (20–30 sec all-out, 2x/week) stimulate growth hormone and mitochondrial biogenesis
  • Sauna cycling (15–20 min post-training) — GH pulse, heat shock protein production

Hormonal Support

  • Men: Testosterone of 280 ng/dL is NOT “normal for your age” — it is a deficiency. Optimal for vitality: 600–900+ ng/dL. Free testosterone, SHBG, and estradiol balance must all be evaluated.
  • Women: Estrogen, progesterone, DHEA, and free testosterone are all relevant to muscle preservation in perimenopause and beyond
  • Growth hormone secretagogues (CJC-1295/Ipamorelin) restore the GH axis without risks of exogenous HGH
  • HBOT — stem cell mobilization via HIF-1α
  • Bioidentical hormone optimization

Labs to Order

  • IGF-1 — GH axis proxy, regenerative capacity surrogate
  • Full hormone panel: testosterone (free/total), DHEA-S, estradiol
  • DEXA scan — lean mass, bone density, visceral/subcutaneous fat ratio
  • Grip strength + gait speed — functional sarcopenia gold standard
  • Telomere length — progenitor cell replicative reserve
  • DunedinPACE epigenetic clock — rate of biological aging
  • BMP panel, osteocalcin, NTx — bone metabolism and remodeling markers

Metabolic Pattern — “The Energy Crisis”
Great 8 Pillars: Energy/Mito · Nutrition · Sleep · Hormones
›
◆ Hallmarks of aging most active in this pattern:
Mitochondrial Dysfunction Deregulated Nutrient Sensing Disabled Macroautophagy Cellular Senescence Epigenetic Alterations

Every cell in your body runs on ATP produced by mitochondria. By age 50, most people have 40–50% less mitochondrial capacity than they did at 25. This is the biological basis of the energy drain so many members describe — not laziness, not “just aging,” but measurable cellular energy deficiency. Simultaneously, blood sugar regulation deteriorates silently for years before any diagnosis, driving accelerated aging in every organ.

How This Pattern Works

  • AMPK-mTOR-sirtuin axis failure — when this master regulator fails, mitochondrial biogenesis stops
  • ETC Complex I/III dysfunction → reduced ATP production + excess reactive oxygen species
  • AMPK suppression from nutrient excess and inactivity → loss of mitochondrial biogenesis via PGC-1α
  • Chronic mTOR overactivation → inhibits autophagy → mitochondrial debris accumulation (mitophagy failure)
  • Insulin resistance → impaired glucose transport into mitochondria → energy substrate mismatch
  • NAD+/NADH ratio decline → sirtuin deactivation → downstream metabolic dysregulation
Do These Feel Familiar? (Pattern 4 Indicators)
  • Energy crashes after meals, especially carbohydrate-heavy ones
  • Fatigue that coffee helps only briefly
  • Fasting glucose above 90, HbA1c above 5.4
  • Waist circumference >35” (women) or >40” (men)
  • Brain fog that improves with fasting
  • History of pre-diabetes, diabetes, or metabolic syndrome
  • “Tired but wired” — exhausted but cannot rest or settle

The NAD+ Decline Reality

  • NAD+ is the primary fuel for mitochondrial energy production AND for DNA repair enzymes (sirtuins, PARP)
  • NAD+ levels drop ~50% between age 40 and 60 — this is not metaphorical, it is measurable
  • NMN or NR (nicotinamide riboside) are direct NAD+ precursors — both work; NMN has more recent human data
  • Combine with TMG (trimethylglycine, 500–1000mg) to prevent SAM-e depletion from NMN metabolism
⚡ Female-Specific: Metabolic and Exercise Differences

• Creatine is essential for women — not just for muscle. Creatine supports brain energy metabolism, mood stability, and gut function. Women have naturally lower creatine stores than men and see proportionally larger benefits. Start with 3–5g/day creatine monohydrate (no loading required).
• Sauna after training — post-training sauna (15–20 min) expands blood plasma volume, enhances heat shock protein production, and significantly reduces hot flash frequency in perimenopausal women. Do NOT use cold exposure (cold plunge, ice bath) immediately after resistance training — it blunts muscle protein synthesis.
• High-intensity training protects the female brain — sprint intervals generate lactate, which crosses the blood-brain barrier and is neuroprotective. This specifically reduces Alzheimer’s risk in women, who are disproportionately affected. MOTS-c activates AMPK at the mitochondrial level, similar to exercise — particularly relevant when function is limited.
• Women’s metabolic response to time-restricted eating differs — extended fasting (>16 hours) disrupts kisspeptin neurons in women more significantly than in men. A 12–14 hour overnight window is generally better tolerated than aggressive 16:8+ protocols in women.

Blood Sugar Optimization

  • Protein and fat first at meals — dramatically blunts post-meal glucose spike
  • 10-minute walk after meals — increases glucose disposal by ~30% via GLUT4 translocation
  • Berberine (500mg 2–3x daily with meals) — AMPK activator with comparable glucose-lowering to metformin
  • Consider CGM (continuous glucose monitor) for 2–4 weeks — seeing your actual glucose response to food is a game-changer
  • Time-restricted eating (12–16 hour overnight fast) supports insulin sensitivity and mitochondrial repair

Mitochondrial Support Protocol

  • CoQ10 (ubiquinol form, 200–400mg) — direct ETC Complex I–III substrate; essential for those on statins
  • NMN or NR (500mg–1g daily) — NAD+ precursors, SIRT1/3 activation, PGC-1α upregulation
  • Alpha lipoic acid — pyruvate dehydrogenase cofactor, potent insulin sensitizer
  • D-Ribose — direct ATP resynthesis substrate in mitochondrially depleted patients
  • PQQ (20mg) — stimulates mitochondrial biogenesis (creation of new mitochondria)
  • Magnesium malate — ATP synthesis cofactor, >300 enzymatic reactions
  • B-complex (active forms) — all B vitamins as ETC enzyme cofactors
  • Zone 2 cardio 150–180 min/week — primary AMPK activator, mitochondrial biogenesis
  • Red/NIR light therapy — cytochrome c oxidase activation, direct mitochondrial stimulation
  • Metformin off-label — AMPK activation, mTOR inhibition (see Cutting-Edge tab)

Labs to Order

  • Fasting insulin + HOMA-IR — most overlooked metabolic lab (HOMA-IR <1.5 optimal)
  • HbA1c + fasting glucose — glycemic control (optimal HbA1c <5.3%)
  • Organic Acids Test (OAT) — Krebs cycle intermediates, ETC dysfunction markers
  • CoQ10 serum — mitochondrial electron carrier status (critical on statins)
  • NAD+ levels — intracellular (specialized lab)
  • VO2 max — functional mitochondrial capacity (strongest longevity predictor in literature)
  • Advanced lipids: LDL-P, sdLDL, TG/HDL ratio — metabolic cardiovascular signature

Barrier Breakdown Pattern — “The Leaky System”
Great 8 Pillars: Gut Health · Immune System · Toxin Load

◆ Hallmarks of aging most active in this pattern:
Dysbiosis Chronic Inflammation Altered Intercellular Communication ECM & Barrier Dysfunction Immune Senescence

Your gut lining is one cell thick — a single layer between the outside world and your bloodstream. When that barrier breaks down, undigested food particles and bacterial fragments (LPS endotoxin) enter the blood. The immune system sees these as invaders and attacks, triggering systemic inflammation, food sensitivities, brain fog, and autoimmune patterns. The blood-brain barrier can become simultaneously permeable, creating neuroinflammation. This is one mechanism, four diagnoses.

How This Pattern Works

  • Tight junction protein degradation (claudin, occludin, ZO-1) → intestinal permeability
  • Mucus layer thinning (goblet cell dysfunction) → loss of first-line barrier protection
  • Microbiome dysbiosis → SCFA deficiency → colonocyte starvation → barrier collapse
  • LPS endotoxin translocation → systemic low-grade endotoxemia → chronic immune activation
  • Skin barrier dysfunction (filaggrin, ceramide deficiency) → immune sensitization loop
Do These Feel Familiar? (Pattern 5 Indicators)
  • Bloating, gas, or alternating constipation/diarrhea
  • Food sensitivities developing in adulthood (foods you used to tolerate fine)
  • Brain fog clearly connected to certain foods
  • Seasonal allergies or skin reactions worsening over time
  • History of multiple antibiotic courses
  • Regular NSAID or antacid/PPI use
  • Heartburn, GERD, or chronic reflux

Primary Offenders

  • NSAIDs (ibuprofen, naproxen) — damage gut lining with regular use
  • Long-term antacids and PPIs — suppress stomach acid needed for digestion, B12 absorption, and pathogen defense; significantly increase intestinal permeability and SIBO risk
  • Antibiotics — wipe out beneficial bacteria that maintain barrier integrity
  • Alcohol — directly damages enterocytes (gut lining cells)
  • Glyphosate — disrupts tight junction proteins and the microbiome directly
  • Chronic psychological stress — via the gut-brain axis, stress hormones alter gut motility and permeability

The 4Rs Applied to Gut Repair

  • Remove: Trigger foods, unnecessary medications (with physician guidance), alcohol, high-pesticide produce, identified pathogens (H. pylori, parasites)
  • Replace: HCl (betaine hydrochloride) if low stomach acid; digestive enzymes (broad-spectrum: amylase, lipase, protease)
  • Reinoculate: Probiotic diversity (Lactobacillus rhamnosus GG, Bifidobacterium longum); prebiotic fiber diversity (30+ plant species weekly)
  • Repair: L-glutamine (5–15g daily) — primary fuel for enterocytes; zinc carnosine; slippery elm/DGL; collagen peptides + glycine; butyrate (sodium/calcium) — SCFA colonocyte fuel and tight junction gene expression

Advanced Liver & Bile Support

  • TUDCA (tauroursodeoxycholic acid, 250–500mg daily) — bile acid that supports liver cell function, bile flow, mitochondrial health, and protection against fatty liver
  • Phosphatidylcholine — rebuilds cell membrane integrity in both gut and liver
  • Milk thistle (silymarin) — hepatoprotective; supports Phase 1 liver detox
  • Vitamin A (retinol) — mucosal cell differentiation, SIgA production regulation

Every cell in your body has its own clock — synchronized to light, food timing, temperature, and social cues. Modern life systematically dismantles these clocks: artificial light until midnight, eating at all hours, variable sleep schedules, chronic stress that keeps the HPA axis perpetually activated. Circadian disruption accelerates aging at the epigenetic level and has been independently linked to cancer risk, cardiovascular disease, metabolic dysfunction, and accelerated cognitive decline.

How This Pattern Works

Total LDL cholesterol is the primary metric in standard care — but it is a poor predictor of cardiovascular events. ApoB counts every atherogenic particle (LDL, VLDL, IDL) and is far superior as a risk predictor. Lp(a) is a genetically determined lipoprotein present in ~20% of the population that significantly elevates heart attack and stroke risk — and is completely undetectable on a standard lipid panel. Both tests cost under $50. Both are routinely absent from standard care. The most important cardiovascular risk information is hiding behind a billing decision. Citing: Pencina MJ et al., “Apolipoprotein B improves risk assessment,” JACC, 2019; Nordestgaard BG, “Lipoprotein(a) as a cardiovascular risk factor,” European Heart Journal, 2010.

What if the mono you had at 19 never fully went away — and has been quietly accelerating your immune aging ever since?
Epstein-Barr Virus (EBV) infects 95% of humans and remains latent in B lymphocytes for life. During periods of immune compromise (chronic stress, sleep deprivation, major illness), EBV can reactivate — producing a chronic low-level viral burden that keeps the immune system chronically activated and drain mitochondrial resources. Reactivated EBV has been linked to multiple sclerosis, lupus, rheumatoid arthritis, and certain lymphomas. EBV and CMV reactivation are found in the majority of people with chronic fatigue syndrome who are tested. A normal EBV IgG titer does not rule this out. The question is pattern and trend over time. Citing: Bjornevik K et al., “Longitudinal analysis reveals high prevalence of Epstein-Barr virus associated with multiple sclerosis,” Science, 2022.
What if your risk of Alzheimer’s disease is twice as high as you think — and the intervention window is happening right now, not in 20 years?
Alzheimer’s pathology — amyloid and tau accumulation — begins 20–25 years before cognitive symptoms appear. The interventions that most reliably reduce Alzheimer’s risk (VO2 max improvement, sleep quality, insulin sensitivity, omega-3 optimization, viral load reduction, hormone optimization) are most effective when started decades before diagnosis — not after memory problems begin. Women comprise nearly two-thirds of all Alzheimer’s diagnoses and have a disproportionately higher lifetime risk than men — driven by the intersection of hormonal aging, immune biology, and APOE genotype. Your window is now. Citing: Jack CR et al., “Tracking pathophysiological processes in Alzheimer’s disease,” Lancet Neurology, 2010; Mielke MM, “Sex and gender differences in Alzheimer’s disease dementia,” Psychiatric Times, 2018.
